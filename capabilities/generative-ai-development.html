The problem

The gap this fills.

Foundation models are commoditized; the engineering is everything around them. Turning generation into a dependable business capability means grounding, conditioning, evaluation, and review flows — so output is consistent, checkable, and actually yours.

For technical evaluators

Under the hood.

Architecture, evaluation and operations — the engineering substance behind the offer. Final choices are set per engagement, in the written scope.

Architecture

  • Control layers. Foundation models via API with conditioning, templates and validation layers — control is engineered, not prompted.
  • Fine-tuning discipline. Proposed only with a measured business case; default is strong prompting plus validation.
  • Prompt asset management. Prompts and templates versioned and reviewed like code, with owners and change history.

Evaluation & acceptance

  • Quality rubric. Per-content-type rubrics scored on sample batches, agreed in scope.
  • Regression suite. Re-run when models or templates change — quality is watched over time, not sampled once.

Operations

  • Cost accounting. Per-generation cost logged by template and batch.
  • Quarantine. Output waits in quarantine until checks pass; nothing ships on faith.

Scope

What the engagement covers.

In scope

  • Model selection and cost engineering

Not included

FAQ

Asked about this capability.

Do you fine-tune custom models?

Only when conditioning and retrieval cannot reach the quality bar. Fine-tuning adds cost and maintenance; we justify it in writing or we do not do it.

How do we control quality at volume?

Rule-based checks, evaluation scoring, and sampled human review — configured to your tolerance, with the logs to prove it.

What does generation cost to run?

We measure cost-per-output during the build and hand you the profile. Model prices change; your pipeline makes the trade-offs visible.
