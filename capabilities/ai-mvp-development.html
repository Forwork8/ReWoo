AI MVP Development

An MVP that answers your actual question — with real users, real data, and a build that survives contact with production. Scoped, documented, and yours.

In plain words

The smallest real version of your AI idea, built to actually run — so you can test the value before investing in scale.

An easy way to picture itA pop-up kitchen before you build the restaurant: real food, real customers, real lessons.

MVP
Minimum viable product — the smallest version that genuinely tests the idea.
Pilot
Running it with real users under controlled conditions, with success criteria.
Production-hardening
Making it reliable and observable after — and only after — the idea works.

New to AI systems? Every page on this site opens with one of these summaries — or start with the studio guides, written for beginners.

The problem

The gap this fills.

Most AI MVPs test the wrong hypothesis: a chat wrapper with no users instead of the risky assumption of your business. A useful MVP is a product decision — what to build, what to fake, what to measure — executed in engineering that does not collapse at the first demo-to-investor moment.

For technical evaluators

Under the hood.

Architecture, evaluation and operations — the engineering substance behind the offer. Final choices are set per engagement, in the written plan.

Architecture

  • Scope discipline. One workflow, one success metric, and a written will-not-build list that is honored.
  • Unboring fundamentals. Auth, logging and backups exist even in the MVP — because pilots that work become products.
  • Survivable architecture. Scale-out points documented: what gets swapped when volume grows, and what does not need to.

Evaluation & acceptance

  • Pre-registered metric. The success metric is defined before building and measured honestly — including when it fails.
  • Pilot review. A scheduled session reads the numbers with you and decides: iterate, scale, or stop.

Operations

  • Runbook from day one. Operating the pilot is documented while it is built, not reconstructed afterwards.
  • Clean exit. Repository, credentials and knowledge transfer at handover — you are never hostage to a prototype.

Scope

What the engagement covers.

In scope

  • Product scoping: hypothesis, users, success metrics
  • Web or mobile application build
  • AI pipeline integration into the product
  • Auth, billing basics, and deployment
  • Instrumentation for the real metrics
  • Handover with documentation and repo

Not included

  • ×Feature accumulation without a hypothesis
  • ×Dark patterns to inflate engagement metrics
  • ×Unmaintainable demo-grade code presented as production
  • ×Equity-for-work or revenue-share arrangements

Inputs and deliverables

What you bring. What you get.

What we need from you

  • The hypothesis you are testing
  • Access to real or representative users
  • Design references or brand assets if they exist
  • Constraints: the tools you use, timeline needs, compliance

Deliverables

  • Deployed MVP on production infrastructure
  • Source repository, documented
  • Architecture and handover documentation
  • Instrumentation dashboard for the success metrics
  • Known-limitations memo — written, not implied
  • Post-MVP decision review

Integration notes

  • Web and mobile deployment targets
  • Model providers
  • Your existing systems
  • Payment providers where relevant

Final connector list is scoped per engagement against your systems and permissions.

Process

FAQ

Asked about this capability.

Can you build the full product after the MVP?

The MVP is scoped so it can grow — but whether we scale it, your team does, or anyone else does is a decision the validation data should inform.

Who owns the code?

You do — repository, infrastructure, and documentation transfer at handover. No hostage situations.

Web, mobile, or both?

Whichever the hypothesis demands. Cross-platform where it genuinely serves the test, not for its own sake.
