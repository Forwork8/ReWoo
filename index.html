Illustrative example — not a client case

What one build looked like

  1. The problem. A support queue doubling monthly, two humans, policy scattered across four drives.
  2. The scope. One grounded assistant: cited answers from policy, escalation with context, an unanswered-questions dashboard.
  3. The build. Corpus indexed and evaluated against real questions; handoff flows designed; shadow-run beside the humans first.
  4. The handover. Runbook, dashboards, and a working session — the team operates it; corpus gaps get fixed by design.

No client names, no invented metrics — real references when the engagement allows it, honest labels until then.

Questions

Asked often.

Do you run ready-made AI products we can subscribe to?

No. ReWoo builds scoped systems to order — this website is our own product work, and everything else is built for a specific client's stack, data rules, and team. If a subscription product fits you better than a build, the audit will say so.

How do you price?

Per engagement, after discovery: a written scope with deliverables, milestones, and a fixed quote. We do not publish prices or make turnaround promises in marketing — anything unverifiable stays out of the copy.

What if the build is not feasible?

You learn it early and in writing. Milestone structure and evaluation reports are designed to surface failure before it becomes expensive — including a stop recommendation when the bar cannot be met.

Who owns the code and data?

You do. Handover transfers the repository, configuration, credentials, and documentation. There is no hostage-taking and no recurring dependency unless you explicitly scope support.

Where is the team based?

Dhaka, Bangladesh — an independent studio working with teams anywhere. The current site you are reading was designed and built in-house.

Can you work with our security and procurement process?

Yes — designs are documented for security review, scopes are written to survive procurement, and phased rollouts with checkpoints are the default shape for larger organizations.
