Who we build for

Four profiles. One honest process.

Engagement needs differ by stage — the written scope, the milestones, and the handover are the same for everyone.

Pre-seed to seed

Early-stage startups

One founder, too much work, and no headcount coming.

The profile

You are the operations, marketing, support, and finance departments — sometimes in the same hour. The AI question is not transformation; it is survival math: which recurring work can a system carry so you can sell, build, and breathe.

What this stage usually needs

  • One painful workflow automated, not a platform
  • Costs that match a founder budget, scoped in writing
  • Tools that work with free or cheap plans you already use
  • Honest feasibility calls — you cannot afford a failed build

How engagements differ

Engagements are small and surgical: one workflow, one build, full handover. The written scope matters more here than anywhere — it is your protection against paying for vapor. Expect to be asked hard questions about what is really worth automating first.

Suggested starting points

  • AI Readiness Audit
  • AI Agent MVP

Series A to C

Growth-stage startups

Hiring plans that AI systems could halve — if the workflows are right.

The profile

You have product-market fit and a scaling problem. Every function is hiring, and the recurring work in support, sales ops, and content is growing linearly with revenue. The AI question is which systems to build so headcount grows slower than the business.

What this stage usually needs

  • Support and sales ops automation that holds at volume
  • Content pipelines that keep pace with growth marketing
  • Integration into a real stack: CRM, helpdesk, warehouse of tools
  • Metrics and evaluation — instrumentation, not vibes

How engagements differ

Engagements are milestone-based with your team in the loop — demoable increments, evaluation reports, and clean handover so your engineers own the systems afterward. Scoped boundaries matter: growth-stage teams feel pressure to automate judgment prematurely, and the scope documents push back.

Suggested starting points

  • AI Agent MVP
  • AI Integration Sprint

Established operators

Mid-market organizations

Process debt everywhere, and a mandate to modernize without breaking anything.

The profile

You run real operations with real history: established processes, legacy systems, and teams whose workflow knowledge lives in their heads. The AI question is where modernization pays for itself first — without a rip-and-replace program that eats two quarters.

What this stage usually needs

  • Automation that overlays existing systems, not replaces them
  • Process audits that respect how work actually flows
  • Document intelligence for paper-heavy operations
  • Change management the teams will actually accept

How engagements differ

Engagements start with assessment — the audits exist for exactly this profile — then build in phases with shadow-runs alongside the humans before anything runs alone. Documentation and training are first-class deliverables, because the system has to survive its owner changing jobs.

Suggested starting points

  • Workflow Automation Audit
  • AI Readiness Audit

Scale and governance

Enterprises

Governance-grade systems: audited actions, controlled data paths, and reviewable decisions.

The profile

You have scale, complexity, and a review process with opinions. AI initiatives live or die on governance: data boundaries, action auditability, and vendor processes. The question is which engagement model fits — scoped builds, private deployments, or capability transfer to your platform teams.

What this stage usually needs

  • Audit trails on every automated action
  • Deployment options that meet data-residency and network constraints
  • Security and procurement processes respected from day one
  • Knowledge transfer so internal teams own the capability

How engagements differ

Engagements are structured for your review gates: designs documented for security review, scenarios for procurement, and phased rollouts with measurable checkpoints. Private deployment on infrastructure you control is a scoping option, and handover includes the documentation your platform teams will demand. Commercial structure follows your vendor process — the written scope is built to survive it.

Suggested starting points

  • AI Strategy Workshop
  • AI Readiness Audit

Fit is about the workflow, not the letterhead.

Whatever the stage, the first step is the same: a call about the work.
